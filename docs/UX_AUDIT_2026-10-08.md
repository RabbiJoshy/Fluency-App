# First-time user experience audit

**Date:** 8 October 2026  
**Site:** https://rabbijoshy.github.io/Fluency-App/

The design is attractive and readable, and the basic learning flow works. Profile creation, card loading, flipping, pronunciation playback, and saved known/practice counts worked in the sampled flows. These are the five changes I would prioritise.

## 1. Fix keyboard sign-in after signing out

After studying and signing out, entering initials, tabbing to **Continue**, and pressing Enter triggered a “Perfect Set!” study result while the sign-in form remained open. Clicking **Continue** signed in successfully. Study shortcuts should be inactive while signing in, and Enter should submit the focused form normally. This is the highest-priority functional issue observed.

## 2. Fix resume when stopping on a phrases panel

In Spanish Level 1, Set 1, stopping on the extra phrases panel after **la** and reloading produced a welcome-back prompt saying “last card: la.” However, **Continue set** reopened **que**, the first card. A separate check from an ordinary Czech card resumed at the correct word, so the observed failure appears specific to the phrases-panel case. Resume should preserve the parent word and return to that point consistently.

## 3. Introduce swipe grading once

Keep the card uncluttered: do not add permanent grading buttons or instructions to its back. If equivalent guidance is not already present, show a one-time hint on the first real card the learner reveals: **“← Needs practice · Got it →”**. Keep it visible until their first successful grading swipe, then remember that it has been learned and stop showing it. Explain the same gestures in the tutorial, while allowing people who skip the tutorial to learn them in their first set. The audit did not establish that first-set swipe guidance is missing; check the existing behaviour before adding anything, and treat this recommendation as satisfied if it already works this way.

## 4. Simplify onboarding and form feedback

The tutorial has 13 steps, including detailed explanations of card metadata. A shorter introduction covering reveal, pronunciation, grading, and moving on would get beginners studying sooner; keep the full tour available through Help. Empty initials currently produce a browser alert. Replace that with an inline message beneath the field, and explain the proposed profile fields directly in the form.

## 5. Use a name and birthday to identify a profile

**Agreed proposal, not yet implemented:** replace the initials-only field with **Username or name**, suggesting a username, first name, nickname, or initials. Replace the optional secret word with **Birthday (day and month only)**. The purpose is to prevent accidental sharing of learning progress, not to provide secure authentication. Look up profiles using both fields; when a match exists, show **“Is this you?”** with recognisable context such as the language studied and last study date. Offer **“Yes, continue”** and **“No, create a separate profile.”** Even an exact match must allow a separate profile with its own internal ID, so a collision never forces people to share progress. Remember the selected profile on the device and skip this flow on ordinary return visits; ask again on a new device/browser, after logout, or after browser data is cleared. Preserve existing profiles and progress when introducing the new fields.

## Testing limits

The audit used a separate test profile on the published site, sampled Spanish and Czech cards, and checked desktop and emulated phone layouts. Cross-device sync, secret-word authentication, physical-phone gestures, and Spotify connection were not verified. This was a sampled walkthrough, not an exhaustive test. No application code was changed or deployed.
